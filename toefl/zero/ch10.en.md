# Chapter 10: The Turning

It was the summer solstice. The longest day of the year.

Lexi said the hourglass had to be turned in the very second where day met night. "When the world is at its thinnest," it said, "it splits most easily."

At six in the evening, we went down those stairs for the last time.

The world outside was nearly gone. Looking out the old library windows, the city was just a few scattered patches of color, like a watercolor left out in the rain. The sea was still there. That blue line was still there. I didn't know if it was because the sea was too big for Forgetting to finish all at once, or because someone still remembered it.

I think it was the second.

---

In front of Shelf Zero, four of us stood in a row.

Mio-senpai wore her grandmother's brass key around her neck. Alice hugged the white book and Allison's eight notebooks. Lexi lay on the lectern, its golden eye fixed on me.

I had my guitar on my back.

"Tokine," Lexi said, "before you turn it, you have to decide what goes into which world. Everything you remember will become part of that world."

I nodded.

I closed my eyes.

---

The first world.

I put Sumi in.

The way she waited downstairs, the way she shoved rice balls at me, her off-key singing, her pen-chewing, her nose wrinkling when she was angry. The coral shell, the blue hair tie, *tomorrow*.

I put in the week before the test. The last week she was still here. If the world had to stop somewhere, I wanted it to be there.

I put in the unstaffed station by the sea. That bench bleached white by the sea wind.

I put in my guitar. And the sticky note in the deepest corner of the case.

*If you forget her, go to Shelf Zero.*

Last, I put in a part of myself. The part that would forget words. Lexi had said a wish to remember one person too much has to be paid for with something else. Then let it be paid in words. I remembered ten thousand. A few less wouldn't matter. As long as I still remembered her name.

---

The second world.

I put in these stairs. The names on the wall, the ones that faded the farther down they went, and the ones that grew clearer.

I put in Allison. I put in Shizuku-senpai. I put in that greenhouse and all its white flowers.

I put in the librarians. I put in the things made from words Forgetting had eaten. They would become monsters. Lexi said forgotten things with nowhere to go turn into resentment. That world would be dark. People would use themselves up to remember others.

But in that world, forgotten things would be seen. Someone would go looking for them, seal them into pages, remember them.

I put Lexi in.

"You'll be there," I said, "waiting for someone who can find your name."

I put in another part of myself. The part with no name. In that world, I wouldn't be in Lexi's pages. Because I was the one who turned the hourglass, the one outside all of it.

---

The third world.

I put in the station. The boy on the platform hugging his vocabulary book. The line he'd written on the first page. *I will study abroad. I will see the world.*

I put in school. An ordinary school, ordinary classrooms, ordinary tests. No loops, no monsters, no talking dictionaries. Only people, one by one, memorizing words one by one, then going where they wanted to go.

I put in lots of ordinary things. Night shifts at a convenience store. An umbrella lent on a rainy day. Burnt pastries. A study room after closing. The frustrated look of a girl at the top of her class.

That world would be hard. Because there, no one would remember for you.

But there, everything you remembered would be entirely your own.

I put in the last part of myself. The most ordinary part. In that world, I'd just be a classmate. Headphones on, quiet, working at a convenience store, carrying everything alone. No one would especially remember me.

That was fine too.

---

I opened my eyes.

Mio-senpai was crying. So was Alice. Neither made a sound.

"Tokine," Mio-senpai said. "Where will we be?"

"In every world," I said, "there'll be a you. Just a little different."

"Will we meet you again?"

"You will," I said. "You might not know it's me."

Alice wiped her eyes and glared at me. "...If I bully you in some world, you're not allowed to hold a grudge."

"Okay."

"If I fall for some weird person in some world, you're not allowed to laugh."

"Okay."

"Idiot Tokine," she said. "...Safe travels."

---

Lexi's golden eye closed. When it opened again, it said:

"It's time."

I picked up the hourglass. So small, smaller than my palm. All the golden light inside had fallen into the lower half.

I thought of Sumi. Not the moment she disappeared. Earlier, much, much earlier: the first morning she ran down the stairs and knocked on our door. I opened it, and she stood there, out of breath, smiling.

"Tokine! Let's walk to school together!"

I turned the hourglass over.

---

Sound went first.

Then color. Then the walls, the shelf, the stairs, the old library, the city, the sea.

The world didn't shatter. The world was divided. Like an enormous stained-glass window, pierced through the middle by a beam of light, the light refracted into three colors.

One was the color of dusk. Amber, gentle, carrying the scent of cherry blossoms and sea wind. In that light I saw a girl with a blue hair tie, a school bag on her back, standing at the gate of an unfamiliar school. She was a little nervous, fixing her bangs. She was a transfer student.

One was the color of ink. Black, deep, edged in gold. In that light I saw a staircase going down, and on it stood a girl with a silver-grey braid and round glasses, holding a dictionary with a golden eye. She turned her head, as if she'd heard something.

One was a very ordinary color. Ordinary blue sky, ordinary white clouds. In that light I saw a boy holding a worn-out vocabulary book, standing at the door of a cram school. He glanced up at the sky, then went inside.

Three colors. Three worlds.

I was between them.

I became sand. Very fine, glowing, golden sand, falling from one world into another, into the third, and back again. One grain, one grain, one grain. Never stopping.

Just before the last grain fell, I heard a voice.

From the amber light. Far away, faint, a little off-key.

"...Tokine?"

I wanted to answer.

But I no longer had a mouth.
