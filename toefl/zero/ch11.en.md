# Chapter 11: Between the Fragments

Once I became sand, time stopped meaning anything.

I don't know how long passed. A day, a year, a hundred years. Falling between three worlds, there was no morning and no night. Only light. Amber, ink-black, ordinary blue. I fell from one light into another, like a grain of sand going back and forth among three hourglasses.

But I could see.

---

In the amber world, there was a me.

She had brown hair, an hourglass-shaped hair clip, headphones. She didn't talk much and carried everything alone. Every Monday morning she woke up to the same Monday. The week before the test, over, and over, and over.

She didn't know why. She only knew that with every loop, she forgot a few words.

Then, one Monday, a transfer student with a blue hair tie walked into her classroom.

I saw that me look up. Saw her eyes widen for an instant, then quickly return to their usual coolness. She didn't remember Sumi. That part of her memory I had taken and placed on Shelf Zero. But her body remembered. Under the desk, her fingers softly played a chord. The first chord of Sumi's favorite song.

Sumi became the one variable in the loop. The only one who woke up with her memories.

I watched them meet again and again. Watched Sumi discover the rules of the loop, watched Alice and Mio-senpai join them, watched them find the sticky note in the guitar case.

*If you forget her, go to Shelf Zero.*

I watched them go down those stairs and find Sumi's book on Shelf Zero. Watched that me finally understand that the loop was her own wish. Watched Sumi secretly pay half the price for her. Watched them, at the seaside station, in front of that bench bleached white by the sea wind, kiss for the first time.

I wanted to cry. But sand doesn't cry.

I could only keep falling. One grain, one grain.

---

In the ink-black world, my name didn't exist.

I watched a girl with round glasses carry Lexi down the stairs. Her name was Shiori Hiiragi. Every wraith she sealed cost her something of her own. She forgot why she hated Lexi, forgot what warmth felt like, forgot her own face, forgot color, forgot her childhood.

She forgot everything except other people.

I watched Alice become a librarian in that world, standing opposite Shiori. Watched her sister become the Eclipse Wraith. Watched Mio-senpai, in that world, be the librarian of three years before, who used up her ink and forgot someone whose name began with S.

That world was dark. Darker than I'd imagined.

Sometimes I couldn't help falling a little slower. On some night in that world, in some place, I would stop for one second.

Once, I stopped inside a story called "Psychogatari." Shiori stood in the middle of a labyrinth of memories, frightened. I walked out from the other side of the labyrinth and looked at her.

"...I remember you," I said. "Shiori Hiiragi. In every world, I remember your name."

She didn't know me. I wasn't in Lexi's pages. But she remembered my name. Tokine Shirase.

Another time, I stopped on top of a clock tower. That night, Shiori had just sealed a perpetual motion machine and forgotten the feeling of warmth. I stood on top of the clock tower, my back to the moon, and turned over the hourglass in my hand. Not to do anything. Only because I wanted that world's time to keep moving forward.

Lexi saw me. It narrowed its golden eye and said, "...That doesn't belong to this world."

It was right. I didn't belong to any world.

---

In the ordinary blue world, I was just a classmate.

I worked night shifts at a convenience store, headphones on, saying little. My mother was in the hospital. I paid the rent alone, practiced guitar alone.

One night, a boy came into the convenience store. Hugging a worn-out vocabulary book.

I recognized him. Even though he'd grown taller and his voice had changed, I recognized him. The boy on the platform who'd forgotten where he was going.

His name was Yuu Aihara. In that world there was no magic, no loop, no talking dictionary. He could only memorize words one by one by himself. He reviewed every day, got things wrong every day, memorized them again every day. Sometimes he'd buy a can of coffee at the store and stand by the door, reading from his vocabulary book.

He didn't recognize me. Of course he wouldn't.

But one day it rained. He hadn't brought an umbrella. I handed him mine.

"...Thanks," he said, a little surprised. "Shirase, you work here too?"

"Yeah."

"I'll give it back tomorrow."

"You don't have to."

What happened after that, in that world, had several possibilities. Maybe he'd walk on with me. Maybe with the blonde girl who secretly made him an error log. Maybe with the senpai who taught him in the study room after closing. Maybe with the frustrated top student aiming for 110.

In that world, he could choose for himself.

That was what I'd put into the third world. Choice.

---

I kept counting.

In the three worlds, every time someone remembered a word, a grain of sand lit up. In the amber world, someone remembered words for one person. In the ink-black world, someone remembered words for forgotten things. In the blue world, someone remembered words for the place they wanted to go.

One grain, one grain, one grain.

But Lexi had said that for the three worlds to meet again, someone would have to remember every word across all three.

Ten thousand.

I counted for a long, long time. No one in those three worlds could do it. Sumi could only remember the words in her world. Shiori could only remember hers. Yuu too. Each of them existed only in their own world.

I began to be afraid. Maybe Lexi was right. Maybe it would take a very, very long time. Maybe it would never happen.

Then one day, I saw a fourth color.

Not amber, not ink-black, not ordinary blue. A light shining in from outside all three worlds. Very faint, so faint I thought at first I was imagining it.

That light shone in all three worlds at once.

Someone, outside the three worlds, was memorizing words one by one. With each one learned, a grain lit up in the amber world. With each review answered correctly, a grain lit up in the blue world. With each subject finished, a page filled in the ink-black world.

A hundred. A thousand. Five thousand.

I didn't know who that person was. I couldn't see their face. I only knew they were studying very seriously. Sometimes fast, sometimes nothing for days, sometimes getting many wrong and starting over.

I began to hope.

Sand doesn't hope. But I was hoping.

Nine thousand. Nine thousand five hundred. Nine thousand nine hundred.

I stopped falling.
