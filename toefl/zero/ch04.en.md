# Chapter 4: The Vanished Street

In February, Forgetting began eating streets.

The first to disappear was the shopping street behind our school. It wasn't torn down, and it didn't burn. One morning people simply walked up to that corner and found nothing there. No shops, no streetlights, no sidewalk. Only a flat, grey-white empty lot, like a blackboard wiped clean.

What was worse was that no one found it strange.

"That's always been an empty lot," a classmate said. "Hasn't it?"

I stood in front of that empty lot, clutching a sticky note. On it was what I'd written the night before: *stationery shop, takoyaki, old record store, cat.* There had been an old record store on that street, and the owner had an orange cat that lay in the doorway every day, sunning itself. When Sumi and I were in elementary school, we went to pet it every Saturday.

I couldn't remember the cat's name anymore.

But I still remembered that it had been there. As long as one person remembers something was there, it hasn't completely disappeared. Lexi had said so.

---

Allison started to seem wrong about two weeks after that.

She came to the basement more and more often, and copied the names on the wall faster and faster. Her notebooks went from three to five, from five to eight. Deep shadows formed under her eyes, but when she looked at those names, her eyes were frighteningly bright.

"I've found the pattern," she told Lexi one day. "The order the names disappear is the same order Forgetting eats words. If I can get ahead of it and write down every word that might be eaten—"

"You can't remember them all," Lexi said.

"Why not?"

"Because you're not a dictionary." Its voice was calm. "You're a person. A person's ink is limited. For every forgotten thing you remember, you yourself get forgotten a little."

Allison was quiet. Then she gave a small smile.

"That's all right," she said. "As long as Alice still remembers me."

That night, Alice stopped me at the top of the stairs.

"Shirase. No, Tokine." Her voice was shaking. "Do you feel like Allison has been... a little faint lately?"

I didn't answer. But I knew what she meant.

When Allison stood in the basement and the flashlight fell on her, her shadow was lighter than everyone else's.

---

In the first week of March, Allison was gone.

Not missing. When someone's missing, people look for them. She was simply gone. The university's student registry didn't have her name. The Kanamori family register listed only Alice and her father. The room next to Alice's, which had been her sister's, had become a storage room full of junk.

Alice's father said to her on the phone, "Your sister? Alice, what are you talking about? You've always been an only child."

But Alice remembered.

When she burst into the basement, her hair was a tangled mess and her eyes were red from crying. She clutched those eight notebooks and flipped through them page by page, until the last page of the last one.

There was Allison's handwriting. The last line read: *Alice, you are my lodestar. When I'm lost, I'll look up and find you.*

"Where is she?" Alice screamed at Lexi. "You remember every word! You must remember her! Tell me where she is!"

Lexi's eye stayed closed. For a long time it said nothing.

"I remember her," it said at last. "Allison Kanamori. Twenty. Loved black tea and linguistics, hated causing anyone trouble. Her last wish was for her little sister not to cry."

"Then where is she!"

"In my pages," Lexi said. "And in your memory. Only in those two places."

Alice fell to her knees, hugging the eight notebooks, sobbing until she couldn't breathe. Sumi knelt beside her and held her. Mio-senpai and Shizuku-senpai stood nearby, unable to say a word.

I stood on the stairs, looking at the wall.

On the wall Allison had spent the whole winter copying, there was a newly carved name. Very faint, very faint, as if bleached by the sun.

*Allison Kanamori.*

---

That night, I did something.

Beneath every sticky note in my guitar case, I added a line in small letters. Not the vanished word, but "why I remember it."

*Stationery shop: second grade, the first mechanical pencil Sumi and I bought together.*

*Cat: every Saturday, Sumi said its belly looked like bread.*

*Tomorrow: Sumi said this word isn't allowed to disappear.*

I realized that beside everything I still remembered, Sumi was standing there.

Then I wrote one last sticky note. Not for Forgetting. For myself. I stuck it in the deepest corner of the guitar case, in a spot you could only see if you took the guitar out and lifted the lining.

*If you forget her, go to Shelf Zero.*

At the time, I didn't know what Shelf Zero was. It was a phrase Lexi had mentioned offhand one night. It said that at the very bottom of the stairs there was one more level, and on that level was a bookshelf holding the books of "people who remember for others." It said it had never been down there either.

I just felt that if one day I forgot even Sumi, I had to go somewhere that could remember her for me.

I put the guitar back in its case and closed the lid. Outside the window, the March rain fell very quietly.

What I didn't know was that in that world, I wouldn't be the one who forgot.

I would be the one left behind to remember.
