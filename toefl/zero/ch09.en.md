# Chapter 9: The Price

"Let me do it."

When Mio-senpai said that, we were sitting beside the lectern in the round room. She said it calmly, the way you'd say "I'm on cleaning duty today."

"I'm the library committee chair. My grandmother was a librarian's partner. If someone has to become the sand, it should be me."

"No," I said.

"Why not?"

"Because you don't remember Sumi."

Mio-senpai froze.

"The first world exists to remember Sumi," I said. "Whoever turns the hourglass divides what they remember among the three worlds. You could put S into the second world, put this greenhouse in, put so many things in. But Sumi isn't in your memory. If you turn it, she won't be in the first world."

When I finished, the room was very quiet.

"What about me?" Alice said. "I don't remember her either. But I could—"

"Same for you," I said. "And you're going to the second world. To find Allison. You said so."

Alice bit her lip, her eyes red again. "...Idiot. How am I supposed to argue when you say it like that."

Lexi hadn't said anything. I picked it up and set it on the lectern.

"Lexi, you know too, don't you. It can only be me."

The golden eye looked at me for a long, long time.

"...I know," it said. "I've known since the day you first came down the stairs."

---

After I decided, the days became strangely quiet.

I began saying goodbye to this world.

I went to the fifth floor. Sumi's mother opened the door, still looking at me like a lost child. I said I was Shirase from downstairs, and I'd brought some cookies I'd made. She was delighted and invited me in.

Sumi's room had become a study. The shelves held her mother's cookbooks and old magazines. But on the windowsill sat a small glass bottle, and inside it was a coral-colored shell.

"What's this?" I asked.

"Oh, that." She looked a little puzzled too. "I don't know when it was put there. It's strange. Every time I try to throw it out, I can't bring myself to."

I looked at the shell. The summer of fourth grade, we went to the beach together, and Sumi found it on the sand. She said it was coral-colored and she'd put it on her windowsill so she'd see it the moment she opened her eyes every morning.

"Auntie," I said. "Don't throw it away."

"Hm?"

"It's important. Even if you don't remember why, it's important."

She looked at me for a long time. Then she smiled very gently.

"All right," she said. "Then I won't."

---

I went to the study room in the old library. On the corner of the desk Sumi and I sat at every day, there was a tiny character she'd carved with a mechanical pencil. *Sumi.* Beside it, the one I'd carved. *Tokine.*

Both were still there. I traced them with my finger.

I went to the station and sat on that bench and waited all afternoon. Not for anyone. I just wanted to watch people come and go one more time. The boy with the vocabulary book didn't appear. I hoped he remembered where he was going.

I went to the greenhouse. Mio-senpai was watering. I sat on the steps and watched her water every white flower.

"Tokine," she said. "In another world, will I still remember you?"

"Probably not," I said. "Lexi says whoever turns the hourglass isn't whole in any world."

"Then let me say this now." Mio-senpai set down the watering can and sat beside me. "No matter what world it is, if I meet a girl who makes me feel like I've seen her somewhere, I'll be a little kinder to her."

"That's it?"

"That's it." She smiled. "I'm the library committee chair. I don't make extravagant promises."

Alice was the last.

She was waiting for me at the top of the stairs, arms crossed, deliberately not looking at me.

"I'm not here to see you off," she said. "I'm just telling you your pronunciation still has a lot of problems. You can't tell R from L, and you say every th like an s. When you go to another world, practice properly."

"Okay."

"And..." Her voice dropped. "Thank you. For giving Allison a place."

"Alice."

"What."

"You'll have a place too."

She finally turned to look at me. Her blue eyes were full of tears, but she glared at me with all her strength so they wouldn't fall.

"Idiot Tokine," she said. "...I'll miss you. Even if I don't remember you, I'll miss you."

---

On the last night, I played guitar in the basement.

The round walls carried the sound around and around. I played Sumi's favorite song. The one she always sang off-key. I played it slowly, very slowly, as if afraid that once I finished, there'd be no more.

Lexi listened quietly. When I finished, it said:

"You know, after you turn the hourglass, you'll forget some things too."

"I know."

"Maybe this song. Maybe this basement. Maybe me."

"And Sumi?"

"Not her," Lexi said. "The first world is made from the part of you that remembers her. As long as that world exists, you won't forget her completely. But..."

"But?"

"In that world, the way you remember her may hurt. You may lose her again and again, and remember her again and again."

I put the guitar in its case. I lifted the lining and looked at the sticky note.

*If you forget her, go to Shelf Zero.*

Then I put it back and closed the lid.

"Lexi."

"Mm."

"In the second world, make sure a good person finds you."

"...I'll try."

"And your name. I don't know what it is. But I'll make sure someone finds it for you."

The golden eye went wet. This time, I knew it wasn't dust.

"Tokine," it said. "Do you know who you look like right now?"

"Who?"

"The first person who ever came down these stairs."
